00The read

What’s right, and the three things I’d change before posting

The strategy is correct and I would not re-argue it. One campaign, one promise, three pillars, franchises as programming rather than campaigns — that is how durable brands are actually built, and the parent-priority evidence supports the position.

But campaigns like this do not fail at the strategy layer. They fail in week five, when the content bank runs dry and someone posts “Our students had an amazing opportunity today!” because there was nothing else in the queue. Everything below is built to prevent that specific Tuesday.

Three changes first.

1. Baseline the survey before the first post

The plan says brand association should move materially upward in six months. Without a pre-launch reading among families who are actively cross-shopping, there is no denominator and the result is unfalsifiable either way. This is the highest-leverage item in the entire build and the easiest one to skip. It happens in Week 0 or the measurement section is decorative.

2. Add a paid layer

The strategy is entirely organic. The stated goal — shifting a category association among families who do not follow the school yet — is a reach goal, and organic reach to non-followers is unreliable and structurally declining. A small, geo-fenced spend behind the two strongest proven assets each month is what converts a good feed into a category position. Without it, the campaign will move the people already inside the community and that will get misread as success.

3. The promise is a category argument, not an Orchard Lake argument

High school should prepare you for all of it is true, well-put, and available to any school in the country. Nothing in the line is ownable. What makes it Orchard Lake’s is the proof underneath it: the lake, the 1885 brick, the chapel, the dormitory at 6:45 a.m., The Summit, a named faculty member, an alum three years out. So it becomes a hard rule rather than an aspiration — every post carries at least one thing no competitor could shoot.

Before anything goes external

Pin both EdChoice figures to the exact table and page they came from. An internal strategy deck can carry a summary; a public claim in a school’s marketing cannot. If a number cannot be sourced in one click, it does not appear in a caption.

01Build order

Two weeks of silent build, then thirteen weeks on air

The single most common way this goes wrong is launching on an empty tank — posting the manifesto film on day one with nothing behind it, then improvising for twelve weeks. The campaign does not start when the first post goes up. It starts when three weeks of bank exists.

Keep posting business-as-usual through Week 0. Do not go dark and do not tease. Nothing about the current feed changes until launch day.

02Asset engine

The bottleneck is capture, not posting

Everyone building a campaign like this instinctively designs the posting calendar first. That is the wrong end. Posting is an hour a day. Capture is the constraint, and three photographers shooting to an event calendar will not produce this campaign — they will produce coverage of events, which is what the school already has.

The operational change is one sentence: photographers stop covering events and start filling briefs. A brief is issued Thursday for the following week. It names the theme, five specific shot assignments, the Wednesday Summit question, and which two of those five are banking rather than posting.

Standing weekly capture quota

Each franchise carries a fixed weekly requirement so the bank fills faster than it drains. Roughly a third of what is captured each week is deliberately banked, not posted.

Roles

Six named people. Not six committees.

  • Brief owner — comms director. Issues Thursday’s brief. Owns the theme sequence. One person, permanently.
  • Three photographers — assigned by franchise, not by event. One owns Prove It and The Work; one owns Summit and Adult Who Noticed; one owns This Is Orchard Lake and overflow.
  • Editor — single pair of hands on every cut, which is how visual grammar actually stays consistent. Monday is edit day.
  • Summit liaison — supplies the question list a term ahead and flags the weeks worth shooting.
  • Admissions liaison — owns the activation slots only. Has no say over the brand slots.
  • Approver — one person, 24-hour service level, four hours on Wednesdays. Not a committee, or the Summit turn is dead by week three.

The bank rule

Never drop below fourteen days of queued, approved posts. When the bank hits fourteen days, the following week’s posting volume is cut — not the capture quota. Reducing output is recoverable. Posting filler is not, because filler is what teaches the audience the campaign was only a slogan.

03Weekly slate

A fixed slate, because seventy-thirty does not schedule

A 70/30 brand-to-activation split is the right instinct and an unusable instruction. Nobody can execute a percentage on a Tuesday. Convert it to a fixed weekly slate with named slots, and the ratio falls out on its own — the activation slots are simply the ones admissions is allowed to claim during the two activation windows.

That is roughly twelve original assets a week, which three photographers and one editor can genuinely sustain across a term. The temptation will be twenty-five. Twenty-five is how you get to filler by Halloween.

What each channel is actually for

  • Instagram — the main theater. Both audiences, prospective students and their parents, in one place.
  • TikTok — student reality. Lower polish is a feature. If it looks like the Instagram cut, it will not work.
  • Facebook — parents, grandparents, alumni. Outcomes and tradition travel here; student humor does not.
  • YouTube — the destination for a family who is seriously considering and wants six minutes of evidence.
  • LinkedIn — capability credibility to an adult audience. Also where faculty and partner organizations reshare, which is free reach into exactly the right households.
  • X — no strategic energy unless there is a specific communications reason.
04Construction

Question, human, proof, brand — with hard constraints

The four-beat structure is right. On its own it is still too loose to hand to three people and expect consistency, so it gets constraints that can be checked in ten seconds by someone who is not a creative director.

  • The question appears on screen in the first three words. No voiceover run-up, no logo bumper, no title card before it.
  • No adjective in the first five seconds. Not one.
  • The resolution line appears in every brand post, unchanged. Never post an unresolved question — an open what happens next? with no answer reads as anxiety, which is the one way this platform can turn against the school.
  • At least one thing only Orchard Lake could show. The lake, the brick, the chapel, the crest, the dorm, The Summit, a named face.
  • Caption is two sentences or fewer before the fold. No hashtag block.
  • CTAs appear only in the final frame, and only in an activation slot.
  • Belonging posts carry no question and no resolution line. They are the other half of the argument — they answer will I want to live here for four years, and dressing them in campaign furniture flattens both halves.
High school should prepare you for all of it.

Launch filmSlot A · Wk 01 · Mon 14 Sep · 60s

“What do you do when being honest costs you something?”

Knowing the right answer is different from choosing it when it costs you something. What happens next?

This Is Orchard LakeSlot F · Friday · carousel, 7 stills

Seven frames from one Friday. No type on any image. No question, no resolution line, no crest lockup. Lake, hallway, a bus, a dirty uniform, a full lunch table, the last ten seconds before kickoff, the walk back.

Friday. October. Orchard Lake.

The contrast that matters. Everything above is advertising. “Our students had an amazing opportunity today to visit XYZ Company! Thank you to everyone who made this wonderful experience possible!” is an institutional newsletter accidentally posted to Instagram. The difference is not budget or talent. It is whether a rule stopped it.

06Summit pipeline

8:27 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., every Wednesday

The Summit is the strongest single asset in the campaign because it is the one thing here that is genuinely hard to copy: evidence that the school deliberately talks with adolescents about consequential things, in a room, every morning. Handled wrong it becomes motivational clips, which is worth nothing. Handled right it makes faith legible as an operating system for action rather than a crucifix with a quotation over it.

The whole thing depends on a same-day turn, and a same-day turn depends entirely on removing approval friction in advance.

16:00Live on Instagram and Facebook. Stories carry the question alone, no video.

Three rules that keep it from turning into content

Never post the whole talk. Never add music. Never let the caption summarize the message — the caption turns it back on the viewer, which is the entire mechanism.

07Shot list

Documentary grammar, and the shots that are banned

The visual system is doing two jobs at once: accumulating recognition through five repeated distinctive assets, and making a parent feel they briefly entered the building. The banned list matters as much as the standing list, because school photography defaults are strong and they will reassert themselves within about a month unless they are written down.

Standing list — get every week

  • Hands working — tools, instruments, pens, equipment
  • A face mid-thought, not mid-smile
  • A teacher leaning into a conversation
  • Marked-up paper, actual handwriting, red ink
  • Students walking between buildings, weather visible
  • A dirty uniform after, not a clean one before
  • One student alone, preparing
  • A group arguing through a problem
  • The quiet thirty seconds before something begins
  • The lake or the 1885 brick in frame at least once per post

Banned — do not shoot, do not post

  • Posed lineups, arms around shoulders, everybody smiling at the lens
  • Thumbs up. Peace signs. Waving at the camera.
  • Students staring at laptops as a stand-in for “academics”
  • Empty hallway “aesthetic” filler with no person in it
  • The podium-and-microphone wide shot
  • A crucifix with a generic quotation over it
  • Cheque presentations, ribbon cuttings, grip-and-grins
  • Any image that needs a caption to explain what is happening

The five distinctive assets

These recur until they are recognized without the logo. That is the whole point of distinctive assets, and it takes longer than anyone is comfortable with.

  • OLS red — as a system colour on rules, labels and type. Not as full-bleed red templates.
  • The crest — small, lower right, an authentication mark rather than a headline.
  • The lake and campus horizon — place is part of the product and competitors cannot rent it.
  • Historic brick and chapel architecture — permanence, which is what “1885” actually buys.
  • WHAT HAPPENS NEXT? typography — one weight, one case, one position. Every time.
08Governance

Three tests and one kill rule

Consistency at this level is not a matter of taste or talent. It is a matter of having a short, checkable rule that a busy person can apply at 4:45 on a Thursday without convening anyone.

Adjective

Read the first five seconds. If it contains excellent, dedicated, amazing, incredible, proud, vibrant, transformative or wonderful, it does not run. Everyone owns those words, which means nobody does.

Attributability

Cover the crest. Could this be any other school? If yes, it does not run. The promise is a category argument; only the proof is Orchard Lake’s.

Newsletter

Does it thank someone, announce an opportunity, or celebrate the school’s own generosity? Then it is an internal communication. Send it internally.

The kill rule

If a post could run on a competitor’s feed with only the logo swapped, it does not run at all. Not reworked, not softened — killed. The bank exists so that killing a post costs nothing.

Stop doing

  • Thank-you posts and recognition posts — move to email and the parent portal
  • Multi-photo event dumps — the seven best frames or none
  • Quote cards on branded backgrounds
  • Holiday and birthday template graphics
  • Any sentence beginning “We are proud to…”
  • Announcing the campaign. The campaign is demonstrated, never introduced.

Tone

The school should sound confident enough not to praise itself. We are proud to develop exceptional leaders becomes Somebody eventually stops reminding you. Our dedicated faculty prepare students for success becomes One day the teacher isn’t in the room. The second version in each pair is a serious brand. The first is school marketing, and every family has already learned to skip it.

09Measurement

Four numbers weekly, one number that decides it

Followers are close to worthless here and will be the first thing someone reports. The campaign is trying to install an association in the heads of families who have not yet chosen a school, so the measurement has to run at four levels, and only the last one settles the question.

LevelMeasureReadWhat a bad number means
AttentionNon-follower reach share, video completion, saves, sharesWeeklyThe creative is not travelling beyond the existing community.
InterestProfile visits, site visits, admissions-page visits, repeat viewersWeeklyIt travels but does not provoke a next step. Usually a CTA-placement problem, not a creative one.
ActionInquiries, tour and open-house registrations, application startsMonthlyInterest is not converting. Check the activation slots, not the brand slots.
AssociationAttribute ranking vs. named cross-shop schoolsPer termThe campaign is not yet in anyone’s head. This is the only one that decides whether it worked.

The Friday four

Ten minutes, every Friday, one screen. Non-follower share of reach; three-second-to-completion rate; saves plus shares per post; profile-to-site clicks. Anything else is a monthly conversation.

The most diagnostic of the four is non-follower share of reach. If by week six the majority of reach is still coming from people who already follow the school, the creative is not travelling and the format needs work — regardless of how good the engagement looks.

The survey instrument

Field it in Week 0, again in Week 13, then each term. Same list, same order, same audiences: current inquiry list, current families, and — most usefully — families who inquired last cycle and enrolled elsewhere.

Ask which school best fits each attribute, with Orchard Lake sitting in a list beside the schools families actually cross-shop: academics; athletics; college admission; Catholic faith; prestige; community; prepares students for adulthood; develops independent thinkers; builds character and judgment.

The number that decides it

Movement on “prepares students for adulthood” and “ready for life after high school,” relative to named competitors, six months in. Reach and engagement are inputs. This is the outcome, and it is the only one worth defending a three-year budget with.

10Paid layer

Spend behind what already worked

This is the one structural addition to the strategy as written. Shifting a category association among families who do not yet follow the school is a reach problem, and organic distribution will not solve a reach problem on its own.

Four rules, whatever the budget turns out to be.

  • Never boost a new asset. Promote only proven organic performers — the top two each month by completion rate and saves. Paid amplifies a signal; it does not create one.
  • Two assets a month, not ten. Concentration is what produces frequency, and frequency is what produces recognition.
  • Geo-fence tightly around the actual catchment, plus lookalikes built from the inquiry list rather than from page followers.
  • Retarget site visitors only during activation windows. Outside those windows, paid runs brand-side and carries no CTA at all.

Split the money roughly three-to-one brand to activation, mirroring the organic slate. The instinct under budget pressure will be to move everything to application-deadline retargeting, which buys the applications that were already coming and none of the ones that were not.

11Failure modes

Where this actually breaks

  1. The product doesn’t get builtThe campaign is a window into a differentiated education. If The Summit, the mentoring, the public presentation, the real work experiences and eventually a graduate record are not operationalized, this is a slogan with good typography — and families detect that faster than schools expect. Cristo Rey can say The School That Works because it built the work system first.
  2. Week fiveThe bank empties, the brief stops being issued, and event coverage returns. Guard: the fourteen-day bank rule, and cutting posting volume rather than posting filler.
  3. The approver goes on holidayThe Wednesday same-day turn dies, The Summit becomes a Friday post, and the strongest asset in the campaign loses the thing that made it feel live. Name a deputy in Week 0.
  4. A second line appearsSomeone adds a tagline for the gala, the capital campaign or athletics by Thanksgiving, and the architecture quietly reverts to eight competing messages. The line is fixed for three years or it is not a campaign.
  5. Consent gapsResidential and international students turn out not to be cleared, and the most differentiating content in the whole system silently disappears from the feed. This is why the audit is a Week 0 blocker, not a Week 4 task.
  6. Victory by follower countReach looks healthy, followers climb, nobody fields the survey, and in year two there is no evidence to defend the budget with. The baseline in Week 0 is the insurance policy.
Build the proof. Then show it every day.
